Engineering project / Design & controls

Iterating a small mechanism until it lifts reliably

Designed and refined a printed gear-and-rack mechanism around a 50 g lifting target, using physical behavior to drive revisions.

Context
Engineering project
Period
2025
Project status
Built & iterated
Conceptual illustration

The engineering problem

The first mechanism had to overcome more than the nominal load: friction, axle slip, support motion, and printed-part clearances all affected performance.

My contribution

  • Revised the effective gear ratio from 2:1 to 5:1.
  • Increased axle diameter from 3 mm to 5 mm and added axle caps to improve alignment and reduce slipping.
  • Enlarged the load plate and adjusted tolerances.
  • Reinforced the assembly and tuned friction and support behavior.

Outcome & current scope

Built and iterated a printed lifting mechanism around a 50 g design target, improving gearing, axle retention, alignment, and support behavior.

What this taught me

A gear ratio calculation is a starting point. The printed assembly exposes friction and stiffness issues that need physical iteration.
